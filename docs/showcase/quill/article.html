Core Web Vitals, explained without the jargon

By the Quill team · 8 min read · Nov 2026

Core Web Vitals are three numbers Google uses to judge whether a page feels fast. They sound technical, but the ideas behind them are simple: how fast does the biggest thing appear, how quickly does the page respond when you interact, and does the layout jump around.

The three metrics are LCP (Largest Contentful Paint), INP (Interaction to Next Paint), and CLS (Cumulative Layout Shift). Each maps to a real feeling a user has, and each has a handful of high-leverage fixes.

The three numbers

LCP measures when the main content finishes loading. If your hero image or headline text takes three seconds to appear, your LCP is poor. Fix it by prioritizing the largest element, compressing images, and preloading critical resources.

INP replaced FID. It measures how fast the page responds when you tap or click. Long JavaScript tasks are the usual culprit — break up heavy work, defer non-critical scripts, and keep the main thread free.

CLS measures layout stability. If content shifts after load (images without dimensions, fonts that swap in late, dynamic banners), the score suffers. Always reserve space for images and load fonts without a flash.

The short version: optimize the largest element, keep the main thread free, and reserve space for everything that loads late. Those three moves cover most of the real-world gains.

Measure before you optimize. Run the audit, find the failing metric, fix the root cause, and re-measure. Blind optimization wastes effort; targeted fixes compound.
