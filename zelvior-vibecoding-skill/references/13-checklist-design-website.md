# Website Checklists (checklist.design — Website)

27 checklists. Source: https://www.checklist.design/website. Every item below is a required test. Use the checklist that matches the page you are building; a page is only "done" when every item on its checklist passes. Do not skip an item because the page feels simple.

## 1. Security
Security and trust page for a website.
- **Certifications and compliance** — Logos and names of certifications held (SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR, HIPAA). Certification logos should be paired with an explanation of what they cover.
- **Data encryption** — Plain-language explanation of how data is encrypted at rest and in transit.
- **Data residency** — Where customer data is stored and processed, with any regional options available.
- **Access controls** — Who within the company can access customer data, under what conditions, and the controls limiting that access.
- **Vulnerability disclosure** — How security issues are reported and handled (dedicated email, bug bounty programme, responsible disclosure policy).
- **Incident history** — A record of past disclosed security incidents, with dates and resolution summaries.
- **Penetration testing** — Details of third-party audits or penetration tests, the most recent date, and a way to request a report.

## 2. Event Page
A page promoting a specific event — conference, webinar, workshop, meetup — driving registrations.
- **Event name and date** — Name and timing shown prominently at the top. Include the timezone explicitly for online events.
- **Event description** — What the event is, who it is for, and what attendees get from it.
- **Speakers, presenters or hosts** — Names, photos, brief bios. Speaker credibility is often the deciding factor for registering and tends to be the most-read section.
- **Agenda or schedule** — A session-by-session or time-slot breakdown of what will happen.
- **Registration CTA** — Clear form or button to register, with pricing if applicable. Flagging ticket availability near the CTA communicates scarcity.
- **Location or platform details** — Venue address and travel information for in-person events; the platform in use for online events.

## 3. About
Tells the story of the company — who built it, why, and what they believe.
- **Origin story** — A genuine account of why the company was founded and the problem it set out to solve.
- **Mission or values** — What the company stands for and how it makes decisions, stated plainly.
- **Team** — The people behind the product (names, roles, faces) making the company feel human.
- **Milestones or traction** — Key moments in the company's history (founding year, customer count, notable launches) that establish credibility.
- **Investors or backers** — Logos or names of investors, accelerators, or notable backers where relevant.
- **CTA** — A clear next step: view open roles, read the blog, or try the product.

## 4. Privacy / Legal
Page covering the legal terms of using the product (privacy policy, terms of service, cookie policy), written clearly and kept current.
- **Privacy policy** — Clear document on what data is collected, how it is used, how long it is kept, and how users can request deletion. A plain-language summary at the top is an option.
- **Terms of service** — The agreement governing use (user responsibilities, acceptable use, limitation of liability).
- **Cookie policy** — Clear explanation of which cookies are used, what they do, and how users control them. Cookie policies separated from the privacy policy are increasingly what users search for.
- **Last updated date** — A clear timestamp on every legal document so users know they are reading the current version.
- **Version history or changelog** — For engaged products, a log of what changed between policy versions. A plain-English summary of changes at the top of an updated policy is far more reassuring than only changing the date.
- **Contact for legal queries** — A dedicated email or address for data, privacy, or legal enquiries as most regulations require.

## 5. Features
Walks through the full capabilities of the product so prospects understand what it does in depth.
- **Feature grouping** — Capabilities organised into logical themes or categories so the page is scannable and not a wall of bullets. Group by the job the user accomplishes, not by how the product is built internally.
- **Feature descriptions** — Each feature explained in one or two sentences from the user's perspective: what they can do, not what the system does.
- **Feature visuals** — Screenshots, GIFs, or short video clips showing each feature in action. Animated GIFs can tell the story of complex interactions better than static screenshots.
- **Benefits framing** — Each feature connected explicitly to an outcome or benefit, not just the capability.
- **Social proof per feature** — A relevant customer quote placed alongside the feature it relates to.
- **CTA** — A conversion point at the bottom and optionally inline throughout, for visitors convinced mid-page.

## 6. Testimonials
A page dedicated to social proof: customer quotes, reviews, and success stories in one place.
- **Customer quotes** — Direct quotes from real customers, attributed with name, role, and company. Quotes mentioning a concrete outcome are far more persuasive than generic praise.
- **Customer details** — Name, role, company, and photo for each testimonial, since anonymised quotes carry much less weight. A recognisable company logo multiplies credibility.
- **Use case variety** — Testimonials representing different customer types, industries, and use cases so visitors can find someone like them.
- **Video testimonials** — Short video clips from customers willing to speak on camera.
- **Review platform badges** — Aggregate scores from G2, Trustpilot, Capterra, or similar, linking to verified reviews. Third-party scores carry more weight than self-selected quotes.

## 7. Affiliate
Invites potential affiliates or partners to join a programme, explaining how it works and what they earn.
- **Programme overview** — Clear explanation of what the programme involves and who it is designed for.
- **Commission or reward structure** — Exactly what affiliates earn (percentage, flat fee, recurring commission) stated plainly. Ambiguous commission structures are the most common reason affiliates leave without applying.
- **How it works** — A simple step-by-step breakdown of joining, sharing, and earning. Screenshots help when a third party connects the flow.
- **Resources provided** — What materials are supplied (links, banners, copy, tracking) to help affiliates succeed.
- **Social proof** — Testimonials or earnings examples from existing affiliates benefiting from the programme.
- **Application or signup CTA** — A clear form or button to join the programme.

## 8. Coming Soon
A placeholder page before a product or feature launches, designed to capture interest and build an early audience.
- **What is coming** — A clear description of what is being built, specific enough to generate interest without overpromising.
- **Email capture** — A simple signup form to collect emails before launch. A tangible incentive (early access, discount, exclusive updates) consistently outperforms a bare email field.
- **Launch date or timeline** — An indication of availability, even approximate. A quarter or season is more useful than no timeline.
- **Social links** — Links to social profiles where visitors can follow progress and updates.
- **Brand presence** — A page that is visually on brand even before the product exists.

## 9. Compare
Positions the product directly against a specific competitor for prospects evaluating alternatives.
- **Clear positioning statement** — An opening that states plainly how the product differs from the competitor. Claiming superiority on every dimension reads as untrustworthy; include honest acknowledgement of trade-offs.
- **Feature comparison table** — Side-by-side table of key features with clear tick and cross indicators. Focus on decision-relevant features, not every feature.
- **Key differentiators** — A section expanding on the areas where the product genuinely excels beyond the table.
- **Social proof** — Testimonials or quotes from customers who switched from the competitor.
- **Pricing comparison** — A clear, fair comparison of pricing structures when it is a meaningful factor.
- **CTA** — A direct prompt to try the product or book a demo.

## 10. Status
The status page for the product's availability.
- **Component status list** — Each major service or component (API, dashboard, integrations, notifications) with its current operational state. A single "All systems operational" row is less useful than per-component status.
- **Live incident updates** — During an active incident, timestamped updates in sequence (identify, investigate, resolve). Cadence matters more than length — a brief "investigating" post every 30 minutes is far more reassuring than silence.
- **Incident history** — An archive of past incidents with dates, duration, affected components, and a brief post-mortem.
- **Uptime metrics** — Historical uptime percentages per component, typically a rolling 90-day window.
- **Scheduled maintenance** — Upcoming planned windows listed in advance with estimated duration and affected components. Announce at least 72 hours ahead.
- **Subscribe to updates** — Email, SMS, or webhook subscription so users are notified of incidents without checking the page.
- **Separate domain** — The status page hosted on a domain independent of the main infrastructure so it stays up when the product is down. A status page that goes offline during an outage is the single most common implementation failure.

## 11. Billing
Where users manage payment, subscription, and billing information.
- **Payment method/s used** — Display payment method details with the ability to update or remove. A subscription service should prevent removing all methods, since that becomes a problem later.
- **History** — A list of past charges with the ability to download invoices. Downloadable invoices should be PDF so they are easily shared in reporting tools.
- **Plan details** — Current plan details including price, features, and next billing date. Include a clear action to upgrade or change plan.
- **Promo or discount code** — A field to apply a code to activate a discount or deal.
- **Additional details** — Information that influences billing or plan decisions, e.g. seats, roles, usage, metrics.
- **Next payment** — For subscriptions, show details of the upcoming payment: amount, date, and method.
- **Cancel subscription** — A clear action to cancel and stop payments.

## 12. Landing Page
A standalone page designed to convert a specific audience, typically reached via an ad, email, or campaign link.
- **Headline** — A single, clear statement of what the product does or the value it delivers. Write for a skeptic who knows nothing and has three seconds to decide.
- **Subheadline** — A supporting line that adds context or specificity to the headline claim.
- **Hero visual** — An image, illustration, or product screenshot that reinforces the headline. A product screenshot or demo video consistently outperforms an abstract illustration.
- **Primary CTA** — One clear action for the visitor to take. Multiple CTAs split attention and consistently lower conversion.
- **Social proof** — Customer logos, testimonials, review scores, or user counts. Placed immediately below the fold, it catches skeptical visitors right as they start questioning the headline.
- **Key benefits** — A concise breakdown of the main reasons a visitor should care, focused on outcomes not features.
- **Objection handling** — Content addressing the most common reasons not to convert (price, complexity, commitment). FAQs are the most effective format.
- **Repeated CTA** — The primary CTA repeated at the bottom for visitors who scroll all the way through.

## 13. Waitlist
- **Email capture** — A simple form (typically just an email field) to join the waitlist, with a clear statement of what joining means.
- **What to expect** — What waitlist members receive (early access, discount, specific feature) and a rough timeline. Vague waitlists generate sign-ups but low activation.
- **Social proof** — The total number of people already on the waitlist, to signal demand and reduce fear of backing something niche.
- **Position indicator** — The user's position in the queue, shown after sign-up and updated as they move up. "You are #847" is more motivating than a generic confirmation.
- **Referral mechanic** — A shareable link that moves the user up the list when others join through it. Referral waitlists consistently grow faster.
- **Progress toward access** — An indication of how close the product is to general launch or how many spots remain at the current tier.

## 14. Press / Media
Provides journalists, analysts, and content creators the resources to cover the company accurately.
- **Company overview** — A concise, factual description of the company, what it does, when it was founded, and who it serves. Written as a ready-to-use paragraph so journalists can lift and paste it.
- **Press coverage** — A curated list of notable media mentions with publication name, headline, and link. Publication logos signal credibility faster than a text list.
- **Press contact** — A dedicated email address for media enquiries.
- **Brand assets** — Downloadable logos, product screenshots, and founder headshots in multiple formats. Light and dark logo versions are both routinely needed.
- **Key statistics** — Notable numbers journalists can use to characterise the company (customers, revenue milestones, countries, team size).
- **Recent press releases** — Archived official announcements for journalists researching the company's history.

## 15. Team
Introduces an organization's staff members, leadership, or key personnel, adding a human element to the brand.
- **Personality and branding** — A theme or tone conveying how serious or funny the team behaves. Colloquial copy makes you appear more accessible.
- **Team members** — A representation of the team, with the amount of detail varying by team size (8 people can be shown individually, 800 cannot).
- **Visuals** — Photography or illustrations of the team, offices, or culture.
- **Group by area or team** — Divide members by area (marketing, design, engineering) to make scanning easier.
- **Link to careers** — After someone sees who the team is and the culture, they may be interested in open roles.

## 16. Cart
- **Item name and image** — Each item shown with its name and image, matching what was displayed on the product page.
- **Update or remove item** — Ability to adjust quantity, change variants, or remove an item directly from the cart.
- **Item variations** — Selected variants for each item (size, colour, style, type) displayed clearly alongside it. A mismatch between what the user selected and what appears in the cart is a common source of disputes.
- **Promo or discount code** — A text field for entering a promo, discount, or coupon code. Showing whether the code was accepted or rejected immediately after entry removes ambiguity.
- **Accepted payment methods** — Payment methods available for completing the purchase. Recognised payment logos increase trust at the most anxious point of the purchase flow.
- **Link to support** — A way to get help with items in the cart, particularly for configurable options or custom orders.
- **Checkout button** — A prominent button to proceed to payment when ready to complete the order.
- **Suggested add-ons** — Related or complementary items suggested based on what is already in the cart.

## 17. Footer
A persistent section at the bottom of a page providing secondary navigation, legal information, and contact details without cluttering the main content.
- **Size and responsiveness** — Define footer width (full or contained).
- **Style** — Colours, font sizes, visual brand elements. Contrasting the footer with the body helps users understand they have reached the footer.
- **Content links** — Visuals, states, and destinations of all links.
- **Legal and compliance links** — Privacy policy, terms, and other legal content are typically located here.
- **Logo or name of business** — For brand consistency and as a link back to the homepage. A tagline or brief description reinforces the core purpose.
- **Hierarchy and layout** — Balanced between logo, links, and additional actions. Group links into meaningful categories only when there is enough content for it to help.
- **Social media** — Logos and/or links to platforms the company is active on.
- **Mobile app download actions (if applicable)** — Links to download at Apple or Google stores.
- **Regulatory and trust certifications** — Badges or logos showing a security or trust measure enforced.
- **Back to top button** — When content is exceptionally long and users are likely to want to return.
- **Contact methods** — An email, phone number, or form for support.
- **Partner logos** — A suitable place for partners to be shared so they are visible on every page.
- **Accepted payment methods (if applicable)** — Relevant for any e-commerce experience.

## 18. Search
A search results page displaying organised findings based on a user's query, in a scannable view.
- **Search box** — The element a user types in. Place it at the top of the page, styled consistently with other search boxes across the site. Include a button to process the search; placeholder text can nudge a relevant query.
- **Visualisation of results** — Show the items relevant to the search. Give as much detail as possible to contextualise results (title, description, thumbnail). Show the number of results. Paginate lists longer than 50. If there are no results, point the user toward another search or pathway.
- **Search progress** — A loading indicator while the enquiry is made, since search may not be instant.
- **Suggested search items** — Offer queries to save typing: trending, most popular, recently searched, autocorrection.
- **Categories and/or filters** — Constraints to help users narrow down their search area.
- **Ordering of search results** — Sorting options, e.g. best price, most relevant, newest first.
- **Highlighting keywords** — Highlight the query terms within the results displayed.

## 19. Careers
Displays job openings, company culture, and employment opportunities, including application submission.
- **Company story** — How the business started and the journey it has experienced. Share the why and motivations behind the foundation, not just the what.
- **Up to date information** — Ensure all content is current, especially quantitative values (team headcount, offices open, time operating).
- **Employee benefits** — Perks and value props for potential employees. Ensure benefits show real, tangible value and are not gimmicks.
- **Teams and divisions** — A breakdown of team members or the areas currently covered. Match the detail to team size — bios for a team of 10, not for 200+.
- **Job openings** — A listing of all roles currently available to apply for. Break roles up by department for easier scanning.
- **Working locations** — Where offices are, and whether remote work is offered.
- **Employee testimonials** — Stories from current employees on why they love working there and what someone can expect.

## 20. Blog Post
Displays a single article's content: title, author, publication date, body text, media, sharing, and commenting.
- **Title** — Copy that educates on what to expect to read, typically eye-catching.
- **Sub heading** — Elaborating on the title and defining the purpose of the post.
- **Thumbnail** — A visual aid illustrating the themes of the content. Thumbnail style should draw from the branding and style guide.
- **Author** — Who wrote and contributed to the post. Make an author profile selectable so users can follow their favourite writers.
- **Content** — The actual blog post. Consider responsiveness and how content is represented on smaller devices.
- **Social sharing tools** — Ability to share or send the post. Positioning this in a fixed position allows sharing at any point.
- **Lead conversion content** — Relevant product information that ties in with the post.
- **Related articles** — Suggested posts relevant to the current post.
- **Opt-in for subscription** — A signup form to subscribe the user to notifications of new posts.
- **Date posted** — When the post went live.
- **Tags** — A specific category for users to explore as a topic.
- **Comments** — A section for discussion that users can join.

## 21. Contact Us
Provides visitors with methods to communicate with an organization.
- **Personality and branding** — Visual character and tone of voice aligned with the brand. Colloquial copy helps you appear more accessible.
- **Clear methods to contact** — Phone number, email, or a form to fill in.
- **Social media accounts** — Users may feel more comfortable contacting via a social platform.
- **Segmenting contact methods** — If there are different contacts for support and sales, showcase that; it shows specific support for their issue.
- **Easy location to get to** — Make this page available in the header or footer.

## 22. Pricing
Presents costs, features, and plan comparisons in a clear, organised format.
- **Pricing options** — Subscription plans or one-off purchase.
- **Pricing features** — What the user will get for purchasing the product.
- **A free pathway to sign up** — A way to try the product before considering purchase. Clearly state the length of the trial period before being charged.
- **Refund or return policy** — A user may want to understand this further.
- **Highlighted price as a recommendation** — Showcase the price most users purchase, or get the best value from.
- **Security logo for payment processing** — Show how purchases are processed with a credible vendor.
- **Frequency of payments (monthly vs yearly)** — Offer an annual option rewarding long-term customers with a discount.

## 23. FAQ
Provides answers to common user queries in a structured, easy-to-scan format.
- **Purposeful information** — Ensure the questions and answers provide value to users making decisions about the product.
- **Contact options** — If a user's problem is not solved, offer a method to contact directly. Order contact options by accessibility — the first should be the easiest, then ascending in level of effort.
- **Table of contents** — When there are many questions, structure topics so users can navigate between areas easier.
- **Search functionality** — Keyword search for users to look up their questions.
- **Navigate by topic** — Segment questions into general topics to structure content and enable easier searching.

## 24. 404
Appears when users access a non-existent or moved page. Communicates the error in a friendly way and helps navigation back to working pages.
- **Logo** — Either the complete logo or a symbol mark.
- **Title** — Make it clear the user is on the 404 page.
- **Description** — Explain why the user landed on this page.
- **Links to other pages** — Offer pathways to stick around: a link to the homepage, a link that allows contact, and a search bar to look for something.
- **Illustrations, patterns, visual flair** — A great opportunity to show off the brand's personality.

## 25. Login
The gateway for users to access personalized features, secure content, and their own data.
- **Logo** — Either the complete logo or a symbol mark.
- **Title** — Tell the user this is where they log in.
- **Account identification** — A unique identifier for the user. Consider multiple identifiers to log in with (username, email, phone number).
- **Password** — A secure, private code for account access. Provide the ability to make the password visible.
- **Link to reset password** — Users may forget their code. Position this link close to the password input field.
- **Link to sign up** — Users may not have an account and landed here anyway.
- **Login via third party** — Turn text fields into one click by allowing connection via Google, Apple, Facebook, Twitter.
- **Testimonial** — Reinforcing the brand and product never goes out of style.
- **Blog post** — An opportunity to show off content relevant to the user.
- **New feature** — A way to build excitement for something new right before a user logs in and sees it.

## 26. Blog
Aggregates and displays multiple articles in chronological order, showing previews, titles, dates, and categories.
- **Hero** — A clear indicator that this is a blog, whether for an individual, team, or company.
- **Navigation** — A logo, links to visit, a CTA, and potentially a search function. Use navigational links to show sub-topics.
- **Layout** — How sections and posts are structured. Consider what is above the fold on mobile, tablet, and desktop; consider article order (newest first, trending); a grid with multiple posts per row has more density.
- **Blog post preview** — The visuals and text of a post. Typography hierarchy matters — the heading must be clearly seen first. A branded thumbnail sets the mood. Any description should economically explain the post.
- **Description** — Summarise what is covered in the blog, shown in the hero.
- **Tags and/or categories** — A topic attachable to a post for convenient filtering.
- **Search function** — With many posts, search makes it easier for users to find content.
- **Opt-in for subscription** — A signup form making users subscribers of the blog's content.
- **Sidebar** — Additional links and resources supporting the posts (search, popular posts, trending topics).
- **Pagination** — Break content into pages after 10–20 items. Choose pagination or infinite scroll based on what suits the product.

## 27. Sign up
Enables new users to create an account by providing required information through a form.
- **Logo** — Either the complete logo or a symbol mark.
- **Title** — Tell the user this is where they create an account.
- **Description** — Describe the basic features of creating an account and set expectations of what they can achieve.
- **Account identification** — A unique identifier to log in with. Email is most common, as it is also a channel to communicate beyond whatever they do next.
- **Setting a password** — A secure, private code for future access. Include a password strength indicator, password requirements (length, special characters), and the ability to make the password visible.
- **Link to login** — Users may already have an account and landed here anyway.
- **Sign up via third party** — Reduce a few clicks to one by allowing sign-up via Facebook or Google.
- **Current active customer count** — Showing how many people use and enjoy the product can evoke a fear of missing out.
- **Testimonial or social proof** — Reinforcing the brand and product never goes out of style.
- **Blog post** — An opportunity to show off content to convert an unsure lead.
- **Billing plans and trial period (if payment is involved)** — Clearly define the length of the trial period and when they will be charged.
